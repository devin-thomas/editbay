# Writing for the voice

- One idea per sentence. A sentence the voice has to breathe in the middle of is two sentences.
- Say it, then show it, or show it, then name it; never describe what the viewer can already see.
- Front-load the noun: "Transcription comes first" beats "The first thing you are going to want to do is transcribe".
- Numbers: "five point five", "fifteen seconds", "a hundred and five gigabytes". Symbols and code are shown, not read.
- Acronyms the voice mispronounces get spelled ("Q A", "L U F S") or replaced ("loudness").
- Rhythm: vary sentence length; land the key word at the end of the sentence, where the beat will hit.
- Pace targets: 140-160 wpm for teaching, up to 175 for shorts. Measure it from the manifest; do not guess.
- Cut hedges and filler ("basically", "just", "really") unless they are the speaker's real voice.
